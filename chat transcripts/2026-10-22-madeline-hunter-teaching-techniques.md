# Teaching techniques related to the Madeline Hunter model

*Chat transcript, October 22, 2026*

---

**User:** looking for teaching techniques in the same family as the Madeline Hunter model

**Claude:**

The Madeline Hunter model sits in the **explicit or direct instruction** family. These are the closest relatives:

- **Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction**: 10 research-based principles, including daily review, small steps, checking for understanding, guided practice, and independent practice. This is probably the most popular modern successor.
- **Explicit Instruction (Archer & Hughes)**: a very practical, step-by-step version with heavy modeling, prompted practice, and frequent responses.
- **Explicit Direct Instruction (EDI, Hollingsworth & Ybarra)**: a lesson-design template close to Hunter's, with strong emphasis on checking for understanding throughout.
- **Gradual Release of Responsibility (Pearson & Gallagher; Fisher & Frey)**: "I do, we do, you do together, you do alone." It's the same arc as Hunter's modeling → guided practice → independent practice.
- **Gagné's Nine Events of Instruction**: gain attention, state objectives, recall prior learning, present content, guide, practice, give feedback, assess, and transfer. Hunter's sequence maps onto it closely.
- **Engelmann's Direct Instruction (DI/DISTAR)**: the strict, scripted, capital-D version, built for mastery and fast pacing.
- **Bloom's Mastery Learning**: teach, check, correct, and re-check before moving on.
- **Teach Like a Champion (Lemov)**: discrete classroom techniques rather than a lesson model, but they fit easily inside a Hunter-style lesson.
- **Herbart's Five Formal Steps**: the 1800s ancestor of this whole family, with preparation, presentation, association, generalization, and application.

If you want a contrasting structured model for comparison, the **5E model** (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate) is equally formulaic but inquiry-first.

**User:** links to each

**Claude:**

- **Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction**: [Rosenshine's 2012 American Educator article (PDF)](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf). There's also [Tom Sherrington's widely read overview](https://teacherhead.com/2018/06/10/exploring-barak-rosenshines-seminal-principles-of-instruction-why&).
- **Explicit Instruction (Archer & Hughes)**: [The book at Guilford Press](https://www.guilford.com/books/Explicit-Instruction/Archer-Hughes/9781609180416). Video clips of the approach in real classrooms are on the authors' site, www.explicitinstruction.org.
- **Explicit Direct Instruction (EDI)**: [DataWORKS' EDI overview](https://dataworks-ed.com/?p=9310) and [the book at Corwin](https://us.corwin.com/books/explicit-direct-instruction-250450).
- **Gradual Release of Responsibility**: [Fisher & Frey's *Better Learning Through Structured Teaching* at ASCD](https://ascd.org/books/better-learning-through-structured-teaching-a-framework-for-the-gradual-release-of-responsibility-3rd-edition).
- **Gagné's Nine Events**: [Northern Illinois University teaching guide](https://niu.edu/citl/resources/guides/instructional-guide/gagnes-nine-events-of-instruction.shtml).
- **Engelmann's Direct Instruction**: [The National Institute for Direct Instruction](https://www.nifdi.org/) and [an explainer on DI design](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~bgrossen/aftdi.htm).
- **Mastery Learning**: [Interview with Thomas Guskey, Bloom's former student](https://principalcenter.com/tom-guskey-implementing-mastery-learning-3rd-edition), and [his book *Implementing Mastery Learning*](https://www.sagepub.com/books/Book277693).
- **Teach Like a Champion**: [Official site and blog](https://teachlikeachampion.org/blog/).
- **Herbart's Five Formal Steps**: [Britannica on Herbartianism](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Herbartianism).
- **5E Model (contrast)**: [BSCS on the model's origins and effectiveness](https://bscs.org/?p=498) and [the BSCS executive summary (PDF)](https://www.bates.edu/research/files/2018/07/BSCS_5E_Executive_Summary.pdf).

One extra pointer: Hollingsworth and Ybarra designed EDI to operationalize research from forebears including Rosenshine and Madeline Hunter. That makes it the closest modern descendant of Hunter's model.
